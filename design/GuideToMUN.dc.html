01 — The committees
What ECOSOC and UNOOSA actually do
Descriptions adapted from
the official UN websites
ECOSOC
Economic and Social Council

One of the six principal organs of the United Nations, ECOSOC is at the heart of the UN system for advancing the three dimensions of sustainable development — economic, social and environmental. It is the central platform for debate, innovative thinking and building consensus on the way forward.

UNOOSA
Office for Outer Space Affairs

UNOOSA works to promote international cooperation in the peaceful use and exploration of space, and in the utilisation of space science and technology for sustainable economic and social development. It serves as the secretariat for the Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space.

02 — Rules of Procedure
How a session runs
Overview only — the full ROP is below
01
Roll call and setting the agenda
The chair calls each delegation, which answers “present”. The committee then votes on which of its topics to debate first.
02
General Speakers’ List
Delegates give opening speeches stating their country’s position. This list runs in the background for the whole session and the committee returns to it whenever no caucus is active.
03
Moderated caucus
A focused debate on one sub-question. A delegate moves for a set total time and speaking time; the chair calls speakers one by one.
04
Unmoderated caucus
Free discussion. Delegates leave their seats, form blocs and draft working papers together. This is where most of the real negotiation happens.
05
Draft resolution and approval panel
A bloc turns its working paper into a formal draft resolution with a main submitter and co-submitters, and sends it to the approval panel for checking.
06
Debate, amendments and voting
Speeches for and against the resolution, then amendments, then a final vote. A simple majority passes it.
Points you can raise
Point of Personal Privilege
You cannot hear, or need to leave the room.
Point of Order
The rules of procedure are not being followed.
Point of Parliamentary Inquiry
You have a question about procedure itself.
Right of Reply
Your country’s dignity has been insulted.
Speak in third person
Always “the delegate of Kenya believes”, never “I think”. You represent a state, not yourself — even when its position is not your own.
03 — Writing a resolution
Preambulatory & operative clauses
Full vocabulary list in the PDF below
Preambulatory clauses
Set out why the committee is acting: past resolutions, treaties, and the facts of the problem. They end in a comma and are written in italics.
Affirming
Alarmed by
Bearing in mind
Deeply concerned
Noting with regret
Recalling
Operative clauses
Set out what the committee will do. They are numbered, underlined at the start, and end in a semicolon — the last one in a full stop.
Calls upon
Decides
Encourages
Requests
Urges
Further recommends
Structure at a glance
COMMITTEE: ECOSOC    TOPIC: TOPIC TITLE    SUBMITTED BY: DELEGATION

Recalling the relevant resolution or treaty,
Deeply concerned by the problem being addressed,
Noting the relevant fact or report,

2. Requests the follow-up action;
3. Decides to remain seized of the matter.
