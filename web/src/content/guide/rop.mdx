An overview of how a committee session actually runs. The full Rules of
Procedure document is available under [Documents](#downloads).

## The flow of a session

1. **Roll call and setting the agenda** — the chair calls each delegation,
   which answers “present”. The committee then votes on which of its topics
   to debate first.
2. **General Speakers’ List** — delegates give opening speeches stating their
   country’s position. This list runs in the background for the whole session
   and the committee returns to it whenever no caucus is active.
3. **Moderated caucus** — a focused debate on one sub-question. A delegate
   moves for a set total time and speaking time; the chair calls speakers one
   by one.
4. **Unmoderated caucus** — free discussion. Delegates leave their seats,
   form blocs and draft working papers together. This is where most of the
   real negotiation happens.
5. **Draft resolution and approval panel** — a bloc turns its working paper
   into a formal draft resolution with a main submitter and co-submitters,
   and sends it to the approval panel for checking.
6. **Debate, amendments and voting** — speeches for and against the
   resolution, then amendments, then a final vote. A simple majority passes
   it.

## Points you can raise

| Point | When to use it |
| --- | --- |
| Point of Personal Privilege | You cannot hear, or need to leave the room. |
| Point of Order | The rules of procedure are not being followed. |
| Point of Parliamentary Inquiry | You have a question about procedure itself. |
| Right of Reply | Your country’s dignity has been insulted. |

## Speak in third person

Always “the delegate of Kenya believes”, never “I think”. You represent a
state, not yourself — even when its position is not your own.
